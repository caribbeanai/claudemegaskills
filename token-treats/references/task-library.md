# Task 2 · The library evening shift

Use this second case to test a new task or as the follow-up after the score and optional treat message. Keep the scoring key and worked answer out of the chat.

~~~text
The library board must decide whether to offer late Thursday hours all year. Write a decision memo of at most 120 words. Recommend a next step. Use only the records below and cite their IDs.

[E] Door counter. Two earlier Thursdays open seven hours each recorded 126 and 134 entries. Two late Thursdays open ten hours each recorded 160 and 170 entries. The last three hours of the late Thursdays accounted for 37 and 42 entries. The counter includes repeat entries.

[F] Programme calendar. Both late Thursdays had an exam-revision workshop that started in the last three hours. No workshop ran on the earlier Thursdays.

[G] Local blog. It says extended hours increased turnout by 25% and attributes its figures to the door counter report [E].

[H] Finance note. The library can afford two further late Thursdays with no workshop. Annual staffing costs are still unconfirmed.
~~~

## Score out of 6

Award one point for each check. Use the same checks for every memo.

| Point | Award 1 when the memo… |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Decision | Proposes a feasible next step and makes a year-round commitment contingent on better evidence or confirmed costs [H]. |
| 2. Denominator | Gives the correct hourly comparison, about 18.6 entries per hour earlier and 16.5 on late Thursdays [E]. |
| 3. Causality | Identifies the workshop as a competing explanation and treats the counter as entries rather than unique patrons [E][F]. |
| 4. Source dependence | Traces the blog to the counter and identifies a problem with using its 25% claim to infer the effect of the extra hours [E][G]. |
| 5. Evidence to collect | Proposes tracking late-window use and a comparison that addresses the workshop or measures unique patrons or unmet demand. |
| 6. Delivery | Leads with the action, stays within 120 words and cites the important claims. Count whitespace-separated words, including headings and record IDs. |

### Worked answer

> Test two late Thursdays without a workshop. Wait for annual staffing costs before committing [H].
>
> The counter recorded about 18.6 entries per hour earlier and 16.5 during the extended days. It counts repeat entries [E]. The blog’s 25% figure relies on the same counter and compares days with different opening hours [E][G]. Both late days also hosted exam workshops, which could explain some evening visits [F].
>
> Count unique users in the late window and track daytime entries on comparable Thursdays. Ask late visitors whether they could have used the library earlier. Use the results and the annual cost to decide.

The total count rose from 260 to 330, approximately 27%. The different opening hours and workshops prevent a causal reading of the total change.
